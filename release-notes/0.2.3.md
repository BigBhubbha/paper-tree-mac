Paper Tree for Mac 0.2.3

- A new setup, written like five entries in a field journal. First, a few specimen tags about you: your year in school, your science background, the fields you read most, and how long you like your notes. Circle an answer in pencil and the flask jots down what it heard.
- Explanations now fit the reader. Your answers go along with each highlight, so a 9th grader gets everyday words and a grad student gets the real terms without the basics. Change your answers any time in Settings, under About you.
- A practice run during setup. Double-tap the right Option key and the island opens, triple-tap and Research mode turns on, then highlight a phrase in a sample passage and a real note appears. Each step stamps itself as you do it, and you can skip it.
- Redo setup whenever you want, from Settings or the Paper Tree menu.
- A new explanation card, in the same style as the one on the Paper Tree website. It is a clean note card with the Paper Tree sprout at the top, the version you asked for (Plain, Simpler, or More detail) in the corner, and a short answer in easy-to-read type.
- A mini glossary. Under the answer, a thin line and up to four hard words from your highlight, each with a short plain meaning.
- Nothing gets cut off anymore. The card measures the answer and sizes itself to fit it exactly. Only a very long answer, taller than most of your screen, scrolls inside the card.
- The big yellow title is gone. What you highlighted now shows as one small gray line under the header.
- Settings starts with About you. The server address and app token moved under "For developers", since the explainer is built in.

If you updated from 0.2.2, setup opens once so you can fill in the tags. Accessibility stays on.
